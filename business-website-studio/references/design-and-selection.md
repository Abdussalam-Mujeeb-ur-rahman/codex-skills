# Design and Selection

Use this guide to turn the approved strategy into section-by-section visual decisions.

## 1. Write the Section Brief

Before generating images, define:

- the section's single primary job;
- the exact verified content it must carry;
- the audience state at this point in the page;
- the primary and secondary action;
- the trust signal or objection it addresses;
- available authentic assets and any new asset dependency;
- what comes immediately before and after it;
- mobile priority and expected interaction.

Do not begin from an attractive template and search for content to fill it.

## 2. Research Direction Broadly

Search Dribbble, Figma Community/templates, strong live websites, editorial systems, and relevant sector examples. Research both the industry and the section type. For example, a law-firm team section and a law-firm urgent-assistance hero have different jobs.

Create an internal reference set that identifies patterns worth learning from:

- layout and composition;
- typography and density;
- navigation behavior;
- image treatment and crop;
- trust-signal placement;
- CTA hierarchy;
- responsive transformation.

Avoid copying a recognizable composition, wording, imagery, or brand device. Combine insights into an original direction using the client's content and brand.

## 3. Generate Three Real Options

Each option must make a different structural argument. Possible contrasts include:

- editorial split versus immersive image versus modular grid;
- named expert lead versus team system versus service-led routing;
- sequential journey versus comparison grid versus evidence-led composition;
- restrained institutional tone versus warmer human tone versus location-led authenticity.

Do not present minor color, alignment, or photo swaps as separate options.

Every option must include:

- desktop and mobile side by side;
- real or clearly marked provisional content;
- the intended interaction and responsive transformation;
- authentic branding and the project's actual navigation labels;
- meaningful image treatment, not empty placeholder rectangles;
- a short rationale, strongest advantage, weakness, and asset dependency.

If a design requires unavailable photography, say exactly what is needed and offer a less asset-dependent fallback. Prefer credible business-owned imagery when suitable; otherwise source consistent, properly licensed imagery rather than mismatched generic stock.

## 4. Respect Responsive Composition

Mobile is a redesigned composition, not a squeezed desktop. Preserve hierarchy and meaning when grids stack or hub-and-spoke layouts become vertical.

Account for transition widths during design:

- when the full navigation becomes a menu controller;
- when side-by-side text/image layouts should stack;
- when angled or trapezoidal geometry stops being viable;
- when type, buttons, labels, maps, timelines, and cards wrap;
- when the selected image crop must change.

Define these transformations before implementation instead of leaving them to framework defaults.

## 5. Selection Gate

Present all three options together and ask the user to choose, combine specific elements, or request another exploration round. Do not implement an unselected option.

Once selected:

- record the option and any requested amendments;
- use its generated image as the visual source of truth;
- preserve approved copy and assets;
- do not blend rejected ideas into it unless requested;
- move directly into faithful implementation when the user says to proceed.

If the user identifies a defect, correct that defect across all affected breakpoints and re-check adjacent sections before moving on.
